# Critical Design: Discussion & Sketch Activity

Based on Chapter 4, “Criticism, function, and discipline,” from *Critical Design in Context* by Matt Malpass.

## Discussion Questions

1. **Think of an object you love.** What makes you enjoy it. How it works, how it looks, or how it makes you feel?

   >Think about a favorite mug, a piece of clothing, or something you keep on your desk. Your attachment might come from comfort, a memory, its appearance, or what it says about you. Consider how those qualities contribute to what the object does for you.

2. **Pick an object in this room.** What does its design suggest about who it was made for?

   >Look at its size, height, controls, or instructions. These choices can reveal assumptions about the person using it. For example, a desk with an attached seat allows a particular amount of space for someone's body. Start with a feature you can point to.

3. **Imagine making an everyday object awkward or strange.** What could that change help people notice about their habits?

   >Try exaggerating something familiar. Imagine a phone case that gets heavier every time you check your phone. That change could draw attention to how often you reach for it. Consider a habit you could make more noticeable through a surprising design choice.

4. **Imagine your work starts an argument.** How would you decide whether that reaction means the work succeeded?

   >Think about what you wanted people to notice and why they reacted. For example, a poster about waste might start a discussion about who is responsible for reducing it. Consider what people understood, what they disagreed with, and what their responses could teach you about your work.

5. **Look at something you recently made.** What idea about beauty, usefulness, or success could you challenge in that work?

   >Start with a choice you made automatically. You might have assumed the work needed to look polished, be easy to approach, or appeal to everyone. Imagine changing one of those expectations and consider what that could say or communicate.

## 5-Minute Sketch: Make an Object Ask a Question

Choose an everyday object and something about how we use it that you want people to question. Redesign the object to draw attention to that issue.

The **object** is the thing you redesign, such as a cup, chair, or phone. The **issue** is the habit or assumption you want people to notice, such as throwing things away, sitting for long periods, or constantly checking notifications.

- **1 minute:** Choose an object and a habit or assumption to question.
- **3 minutes:** Sketch your redesign. Focus on communicating the idea; a rough drawing is enough.
- **1 minute:** Give it a name and finish: “This object makes people think about…”

**Example:** Sketch a disposable coffee cup with a chain of used cups attached to its handle. Every coffee adds another cup to carry. This makes the waste from a daily habit visible. Your sketch can be playful, exaggerated, or impractical.

Afterward, share your sketch with the class or a classmate to interpret the sketch before you explain your intention.
